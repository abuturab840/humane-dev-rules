# Intake questions

Answer these ten questions when you start a project. It takes about five minutes.

The "Always" rules apply to every project, whatever you answer. Each "yes" below adds a few more things to do. They're listed under each question in plain words. On the website, point at a rule number to see the full rule.

If you're not sure of an answer, say yes for now. It's easier to drop something later than to add it after launch.

## Do you collect or store personal data?

Personal data is anything that can identify a person, like a name, email address, phone number or IP address. A simple contact form counts.

If yes:

- Let people see, download and delete their data (rule 14).
- Use the higher security level (rule 22).
- Scramble stored passwords and encrypt sensitive data (rule 26).
- Publish a privacy notice that explains what you collect and why (rule 45).
- Have a plan for data leaks, including who you tell and how fast (rule 50).
- Answer people's requests about their data within a month (rule 51).
- If it's health, fingerprint, face or payment data, do a written privacy risk check before you build (rule 3).

## Do you have visitors from the EU or UK?

If yes, and you use optional cookies, analytics or tracking tags:

- Don't load anything optional until the visitor says yes (rule 15).
- Put "Reject all" right next to "Accept all", the same size and style (rule 16).
- Add a cookie settings link to your footer, and remember each choice for six months (rule 19).
- Check your cookies every three months, and each time you add a plugin or tag (rule 20).

## Do you have visitors from the US?

If yes:

- If you sell or share visitors' data or run targeted ads, respect the browser's "don't sell my data" signal and add a "Do Not Sell or Share" link (rule 17).
- If you track visitors, don't record their screens or chats without asking first, and don't put ad tracking on sensitive pages like health, money or account pages (rule 18).

## Could children use it?

If yes:

- Don't use endless feeds for anyone under 18 (rule 8).
- Use the safest settings by default: no profiling, location off, parental consent where the law needs it, and nothing designed to be addictive (rule 43).

## Does it use AI?

If yes, follow the points that fit your product:

- Label replies written by AI (rule 36a).
- If the AI has a human name, face or voice, say clearly that it's AI (rule 36b).
- Label images, audio and video made by AI (rule 37).
- Treat everything the AI reads or writes as untrusted, and guard against known AI attacks (rule 38).
- Give the AI as little power as possible. A person approves anything that spends money, sends messages or deletes things (rule 39).
- A person checks AI decisions about money, health, jobs or legal matters (rule 40).
- Don't track people's moods or use emotional pressure to keep them using it (rule 41).
- Try hard to make it misbehave before launch, so you find the problems first (rule 42).

## Does it take payments or subscriptions?

If yes:

- Never store card numbers yourself. Use a payment provider like Stripe or PayPal (rule 31).
- Write terms that make cancelling clear and easy (rule 45).

## Can users post content, images or messages?

If yes:

- If you rank or recommend posts, reward helpful ones, not outrage (rule 11).
- Remove reported intimate images within 48 hours (rule 51).

## Do you use optional cookies, pixels or tracking?

If yes:

- Follow the cookie points under the EU, UK and US questions above for the places your visitors come from.
- Check your cookies before every release (rule 33).
- Publish a cookie policy (rule 45).

## Is it a mobile app or software people install?

If yes:

- Mobile apps also meet the mobile security standard (rule 22).
- Tell people how long you'll keep sending security updates (rule 47).

## Do users log in?

If yes:

- Set privacy options to the most private choice by default (rule 13).
- Check on the server that each person is allowed to do what they ask, every time (rule 23).
- Test that each type of user can only reach what they should (rule 33).
- Write terms that make closing an account clear and easy (rule 45).

## Also check for these

Some rules depend on features rather than the questions above. If your product has any of these, add them too:

- Notifications: keep them off by default, except essential ones (rule 6).
- Video or audio: no autoplay (rule 7).
- A feed: add natural stopping points (rule 8).
- A big launch, or a product that's been live a year: get a security test by an expert (rule 34).
- Points, badges or rewards: make them earned and predictable (rule 57).
- Invites: people choose who to invite, one by one (rule 58).
- Something people use every day: give them quiet hours, limits and snooze (rule 59).
- Numbers or reviews on screen: only real ones, no fake urgency (rule 60).

## What to do with your answers

Write them down in the [project template](TEMPLATE.md), with the list of rules that apply.

Answer the questions again whenever the product gets a big new feature, such as accounts, payments or AI.
